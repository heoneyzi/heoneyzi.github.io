Study · Multimodal LLMs · 2025–26

Hallucination study

Why multimodal AI sees things that aren’t there

A year of study on hallucination in vision and audio-visual language models, with two small pilot experiments.

26 notes · 2 pilotsAll notes and experimentsMay 2025 – May 2026

1Topic

Multimodal models mention objects that aren’t in the picture or sounds that were never played — often because the language side answers from habit instead of evidence.

2What I did

Studied attention-based fixes for image–text models, proposed a “look like a human” segment-guided attention idea, then set up an audio-visual LLM (video-SALMONN 2+) and ran two pilot tests.

3Result

The model called 4 of 6 mismatched audio–video pairs “matching”, and on questions whose answer was “none of the above” it never chose that option (0 of 9).

VisionWhy it matters to me

Asking where a model looks when it invents something is an interpretability question — the same lens I now use on DNA models.

— Jiheon Kang

01 · BackgroundThe question

Attention — how much each generated word looks back at image, audio or text — is a cheap, training-free lens on hallucination, and most fixes I studied work by reshaping it at inference time. So it is the natural first thing to measure on a new model.

02 · ApproachHow it works

  1. Survey the causesData, model, training and inference — and the families of inference-time fixes.
  2. Propose an idea“Look like a human”: let each generated word choose which segmented object to attend to.
  3. Move to audio + videoSet up video-SALMONN 2+ and log where its attention goes.
  4. Run two pilotsAVHBench (12 videos) and DAVE (50 samples).
