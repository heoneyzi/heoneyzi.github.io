DNA · Model interpretability · 2026

GDTR

Where does a DNA model make up its mind?

A training-free “depth ruler” for the DNA language model Evo 2 — and what it reveals about biology. Follow-up research is ongoing.

Oral · ICML 2026 GenBio WorkshopFollow-up ongoingAuthorApr 2026 – ongoing

1Topic

DNA language models read DNA letter by letter and are already used to judge disease variants — but we rarely know where inside the model that judgement is made.

2What I did

Without retraining, measured the layer at which each DNA letter “settles” in Evo 2 7B, and linked that depth to splice sites, regulatory elements, genes and disease variants.

3Result

Biology shows up as depth: splice sites settle about 2 layers earlier than introns, and the effect carries over to a chromosome the ruler never saw (94.6 %).

VisionWhy it matters to me

A DNA model trained only to predict the next base settles on splice sites about two layers earlier than on introns — grammar that nature wrote, found inside a model. That is the kind of real task I want to work on: reading the genome’s rules through models. Next, I want to see how the last layers turn that grammar into a prediction.

— Jiheon Kang

01 · BackgroundThe question

Evo 2 is trained only to guess the next letter of DNA, yet researchers already use its internal signals to annotate genomes and score variants. If we knew where along its 32 layers the model resolves each position, we would know which layer to read — and which parts of the genome it understands early or late.

02 · ApproachHow it works

  1. Build a depth rulerFor every letter, find the first layer whose internal state already points the same way as the final one — its settling depth.
  2. Read biology from depthCompare settling depth across splice sites, exons, introns, regulatory elements and ClinVar disease variants.
  3. Check that it holdsCalibrate on chromosome 22, test on held-out chromosome 17, and rule out simple explanations such as prediction uncertainty.
  4. Keep going as a teamOur YAICON team (TDiG) widened the ruler into 17 geometric measures; I am now studying what the final layers do.
