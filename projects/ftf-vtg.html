Multimodal AI · Video · 2025

FTF-VTG

Finding when a sentence happens in a video — without training

First-author paper from the deep daiv. multimodal team I led, accepted at the 2025 IEIE Summer Annual Conference.

Accepted · 2025 IEIE Summer Annual ConferenceFirst author · Team LeadNov 2024 – Summer 2025

1Topic

Video search needs the start and end of the moment a sentence describes. Most methods train on costly labels or stack several large models.

2What I did

Led the research team and first-authored the paper: score each frame against the sentence with a frozen CLIP model, then turn that similarity curve into a time span.

3Result

More accurate than a training-free baseline at about a tenth of the GPU memory: DiDeMo R@1 19.30 vs 15.56, VidSTG mIoU 39.01 vs 24.94.

VisionWhy it matters to me

Frozen models already know more than we use. Reading them carefully — here, frame by frame — replaced heavy training. The same instinct now drives my work on DNA models, where what a model has learned can be one of nature’s rules.

— Jiheon Kang

01 · BackgroundThe question

When a strong image–text model scores each frame against the query, the score rises as the described event starts and falls as it ends. If the boundaries are visible in that curve, finding the moment becomes a simple, traceable signal-processing problem.

02 · ApproachHow it works

  1. Score the framesA frozen CLIP model rates every frame against the sentence.
  2. Clean the curveSmooth the similarity curve and find where it rises and falls.
  3. Pick the momentTurn the strongest region into start and end times — no training, no LLM.
