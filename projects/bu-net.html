Clinic · Medical imaging · 2024

Lightweight BU-Net

Brain-tumour segmentation on a student GPU budget

Medical AI team project at deep daiv., presented at the 8th deep daiv. Open Seminar.

Presented · 8th deep daiv. Open SeminarTeam Lead · 3-member teamSpring 2024

1Topic

Radiologists outline brain tumours by hand on four kinds of MRI. Tumours are tiny next to the brain, so a model that predicts “no tumour” everywhere can still look accurate.

2What I did

Led a three-person team that studied BU-Net and built U-Net, U-Net + wide context and a simplified BU-Net on BraTS 2018 MRI slices; in 2026 I restored the archive with a comparison viewer and tests.

3Result

Presented a side-by-side comparison of predicted tumour masks at the open seminar. No Dice score was recorded at the time, so none is claimed.

VisionWhy it matters to me

My first medical project: the answers came from real patients’ scans rather than from a task someone designed — a first taste of the real tasks I now look for.

— Jiheon Kang

01 · BackgroundThe question

BU-Net adds two ideas to the classic U-Net: wide-context blocks that see far across the image cheaply, and richer skip connections. Could those ideas survive when the model is simplified for student hardware?

02 · ApproachHow it works

  1. Read the papersReviewed BU-Net and a chest X-ray preprocessing paper.
  2. Build three modelsU-Net, U-Net + wide context and a simplified BU-Net in PyTorch.
  3. Compare and presentShowed the predicted tumour masks side by side at the seminar.
  4. Restore the archive (2026)Recovered the notebooks and added a viewer, tests and reproduction notes.
