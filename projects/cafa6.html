Protein · Function prediction · 2026

CAFA 6

Reading a protein’s job from its sequence

An international protein-function prediction challenge on Kaggle — our five-person team won a bronze medal.

Bronze Medal · international challengeTeam member · ProtT5 branchJan – Feb 2026

1Topic

Protein sequences are cheap to read, but lab tests of what each protein does are slow — most known proteins have no confirmed function.

2What I did

In a five-person team, took the ProtT5 branch: a protein language model fine-tuned to score Gene Ontology functions, alongside teammates’ ESM-C and JEPA routes.

3Result

The team won a bronze medal, learning from 82,404 labelled proteins and predicting for a 224,309-protein test set.

VisionWhy it matters to me

Every label in CAFA is a function that nature gave a protein and an experiment confirmed. Learning those rules from sequence helps decide which proteins get studied next — and CAFA taught me that how a task is scored, with rare, specific functions weighing more, shapes what a model learns.

— Jiheon Kang

01 · BackgroundThe question

CAFA is a prospective challenge: predictions are judged against functions discovered after the deadline. Functions come from the Gene Ontology — a tree of about 26,000 terms such as “binding” or “nucleus” — and rare, specific terms count more than generic ones.

02 · ApproachHow it works

  1. Understand the data82,404 training proteins and 26,125 function terms — most terms label only a handful of proteins.
  2. Split the routesEach member took one approach: GO retrieval, ESM-C embeddings, ProtT5 (mine) and JEPA pre-training.
  3. Respect the hierarchyPropagate predictions up the ontology tree so that every answer stays consistent.
