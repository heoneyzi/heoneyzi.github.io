Genomics study

Learning biology as an engineer

Two seasons of Functional Genomics study at Yonsei AI (YAI) — the notebook where GDTR and our VCC entry began.

Season 2 Team LeadOngoingMember (season 1) · Team Lead (season 2)Jan 2026 – ongoing

1Topic

I came to biology from electrical engineering. The fastest way I know to learn a field is to read, write it up, argue about it in a team — then test the open questions.

2What I did

Season 1 (Jan–Apr 2026): CAFA 6, then a journal club on genome language models where I presented whether their embeddings beat simple baselines. Season 2 (Jul 2026–): I lead the team through Perturbation biology into the Virtual Cell Challenge.

3Result

The journal club’s last meeting proposed the layer-by-layer Evo 2 analysis that became GDTR; season 2 became our VCC 2026 entry.

VisionWhy it matters to me

Coming from engineering, I learn biology by building on it. Every note is a rule of nature I wanted to understand — and several of those questions became my research.

— Jiheon Kang

01 · BackgroundThe question

How does a DNA sequence turn into what a cell does — and how much of that path can today’s models follow? The notes stay in their original Korean, with every teammate credited by name and a guide that suggests a reading order.

02 · ApproachHow it works

  1. PrimerBiology for ML people: DNA, RNA, proteins, splicing and mutations.
  2. Season 1 · proteins & genome LMsCAFA 6, then five journal-club weeks on genome language models.
  3. Season 2 · cellsFoundation-model limits, drug-response models and VCC 2025 lessons.
  4. Into experimentsOpen questions became GDTR and our VCC 2026 pipeline.
