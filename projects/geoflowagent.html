GeoFlowAgent

An AI agent that plans genomics tool use on a frozen model’s map

Independent research on AI agents for clinical variant interpretation, completed in September 2026.

Independent research · completedIndependent researcherSep 2026

1Topic

AI agents are starting to run genomics workflows: check how common a variant is, run predictors, look up clinical records, then decide. Which tool call brings them closer to the answer?

2What I did

Kept biomedical language models frozen and used their embedding space as the planner’s map; built a synthetic benchmark evaluated once on a held-out test, and tasks derived from ClinGen expert records.

3Result

Act, observe, replan clearly beat planning everything at once (0.40 vs 0.12 on the one-time test, 0.60 vs 0.12 on ClinGen tasks). The “best” distance measure changed with how the space is read.

VisionWhy it matters to me

Interpreting a variant is a real task: the evidence comes from nature, gathered in expert records, and many lookups come back empty. The agent did best when it looked at each result before choosing the next step — the way a scientist works. I want agents that explore biology like that.

— Jiheon Kang

01 · BackgroundThe question

ClinGen publishes which evidence experts applied to a variant — and which they checked and rejected. That makes a realistic testbed: some tool calls cost time and return nothing useful, so planning really matters. The idea builds on FlowAgent’s latent flow-matching planning.

02 · ApproachHow it works

  1. Freeze the encoderUse a pretrained biomedical model’s embeddings as the map and train only a small head.
  2. Plan whole routesA flow-matching planner proposes complete tool-use plans toward the goal.
  3. Seal the testFix the rules in advance and open the test split only once.
  4. Claim only what holdsKeep negative results, and call a result “unresolved” when its confidence interval includes zero.
