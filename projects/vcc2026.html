Virtual Cell Challenge 2026

Predicting how an unseen cell responds to a gene knockdown

Arc Institute’s Virtual Cell Challenge 2026, with a six-person team I lead.

Arc Institute challenge · final test Oct 2026OngoingTeam Lead · 6-member teamAug 2026 – ongoing

1Topic

Switch off one gene and the whole cell shifts a little. Such experiments exist at scale for only a few cell lines; a “virtual cell” could predict new ones before anyone runs them.

2What I did

As team lead, built a training-free pipeline that turns control cells into a full submission — 400 cells × 18,533 genes per knockdown — plus a leakage-controlled benchmark to test ideas honestly.

3Result

Borrowing effects already measured in similar cell lines beat “no change” (0.5794 vs 0.5102 on a public proxy test); large frozen cell encoders added little.

VisionWhy it matters to me

How a cell responds to losing a gene is decided by nature, not by a benchmark designer. Our results show that today’s cell embeddings capture only part of those rules — I want to build representations, and benchmarks, that capture more of them.

— Jiheon Kang

01 · BackgroundThe question

In a Perturb-seq experiment, one gene is silenced with CRISPRi and hundreds of single cells are sequenced. The 2026 challenge gives teams only unperturbed control cells from three unseen cancer cell lines and 300 target genes — no training data from those lines — and asks what the knocked-down cells will look like.

02 · ApproachHow it works

  1. Read the contextDescribe the new cell line from its control cells — raw profiles, PCA or frozen foundation-model embeddings.
  2. Transfer known effectsBorrow knockdown effects measured in the most similar reference cell lines.
  3. Generate the cellsTurn predicted effects into 400 realistic cells per target, ready to submit.
  4. Control evaluation leakageScore every idea on a shadow benchmark built from public data, with the held-out answers sealed.
